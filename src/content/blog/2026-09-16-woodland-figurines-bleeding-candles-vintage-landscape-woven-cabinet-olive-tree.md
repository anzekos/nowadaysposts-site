The rooms people save on Pinterest in September share one quality that is hard to buy directly: they look accumulated. A shelf that seems to have filled up over a few autumns, a mantel painting that might have come from a grandmother, a corner tree that has obviously been there for years. The searches moving fastest this week are all circling that look from different sides. Whimsical fall decor is up 700% in a month and 500% on the year, fall mantle decor ideas up 500%, Halloween house party ideas up 400%, bathroom decor ideas up 300% on the year and living room decor ideas up 400%. None of that time actually has to pass. Below are five pieces, one per trend, each of which does in an afternoon what a room normally takes years to collect.

**Whimsical fall decor, up 700% this month, and the three animals that look picked up on a walk.** Whimsical is a tricky word in decor, because the cheap version of it — glossy resin gnomes, cartoon faces, painted plastic — reads as a gift shop rather than a house. What the saved pins actually show is quieter: small creatures that look made from the woods they came from. This trio is exactly that. A hedgehog about five inches long whose whole back is a coat of overlapping pressed dried-leaf petals, an owl with a crown of real twigs for eyebrows and a fluffy grey belly, and a small grey fox in sisal with a cream-tipped tail curled round its feet. Each stands three to four inches tall, which is the right size to tuck between books, beside a mini pumpkin or under a glass dome, and small enough that three of them never crowd a shelf. Because they are fibre and twig rather than paint, they sit next to wood, linen and stoneware without shouting — and nothing about them says October, so they stay out until spring. 4.7 stars from 273.

**Halloween house party ideas, up 400% this month, and the candles that improve as the evening goes on.** Most Halloween tables fail the same way: a lot of plastic bought for one night that looks worst in the photographs. The cheapest upgrade is not decoration at all, it is the candles. These are matte black tapers, ten inches tall, that run deep blood-red wax down their sides as they burn. Unlit they are simply elegant black candles; an hour into dinner every one of them has its own drips, pooling red at the base of the holder, and the table looks more deliberate than it did when you lit it. There are 24 in the box, which is the real point: enough for every candlestick on the table, a row on the mantel and two in the bathroom, so the whole house gets the same mood rather than one centerpiece. They are unscented, so they can stand right beside the food, and they fit ordinary taper holders — mismatched brass looks best. Put a drip tray or a runner you do not love underneath. 4.3 stars from 134.

**Fall mantle decor ideas, up 500% this month, and the painting that does the job garland is usually asked to do.** Garland is where most fall mantels start, and it is also why most of them look alike. The mantels that get saved start somewhere else: with one old-looking landscape leaning against the wall and everything else layered in front of it. This is a 12 by 16 inch framed canvas of a sunlit forest path — dark greens on one side, burnt orange and amber arching over from the other, a glow of pale gold at the end of the path — in the slightly faded palette of an oil found in a thrift shop. The canvas is textured, so light raking across it reads like brushwork, and it sits in a thin honey-oak float frame rather than an ornate gold one, which keeps it from looking like a reproduction. It arrives ready to hang with the fixings in the box, but try it leaning first, with a tall candlestick and a jug of dried stems overlapping one corner. And because it is a landscape rather than a pumpkin, it earns its place on the wall long after the season is over. 4.6 stars from 798.

**Bathroom decor ideas, up 300% on the year, and the one wall in a small bathroom that nobody uses.** The space above the toilet is the largest empty surface in most small bathrooms, and the usual answer — an open shelf — just moves the bottles up to eye level. A closed cabinet is the better move, and this one does not look like a medicine cabinet. It is 24 inches wide, 25 tall and only 8 deep, so it clears your head, in a light oak finish with two doors whose panels are woven linen with a rattan look, each with a slim brass pull. Behind the doors is a height-adjustable shelf for everything you would rather not see; magnetic catches keep them shut. Underneath runs one open cubby about eight inches tall, and that is the part that does the decorating — three rolled towels, a trailing plant, one bottle without a label. Everything else disappears. It is the kind of piece that looks built in, which is exactly what a rented bathroom usually lacks. 4.6 stars from 457.

**Living room decor ideas, up 400% on the year, and the tree that has apparently always been in that corner.** Every living room has one corner that no cushion, throw or lamp quite fixes, and height is the only thing that does. A real olive tree indoors sulks without a south window and drops leaves across the floor all winter; this one is six feet of faux olive with a twisting, ridged tan trunk that splits into thin stems, an airy crown of narrow two-tone sage leaves with silvery undersides, and small dark olives scattered through it. The part most listings skip is the pot, and this one arrives in its own tall, matte cream planter, eleven inches across, so there is no hunt for something big and heavy enough. The branches slot together and bend, so it can be shaped wide and open for an empty corner or slim beside the arm of a sofa, and the canopy stays open enough to see the wall through it — the difference between an olive tree and a green ball. No light, no watering, no leaves to sweep up. 4.5 stars from 778.

Five trends, five rooms, and the same trick in each one: animals that look collected rather than bought, candles that look an hour into the party, a painting that looks inherited, a cabinet that looks built in and a tree that looks like it has lived in the corner for years. None of it took years. It only has to look like it did.
