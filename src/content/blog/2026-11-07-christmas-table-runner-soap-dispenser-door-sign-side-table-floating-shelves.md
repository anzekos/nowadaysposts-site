Christmas decor searches are climbing fast, and the places people start are the table, the kitchen sink and the front door. Today's picks cover those three with small, low-effort pieces, then add two fixes that help a small living room all year.

**A Christmas table runner for the dining table.** A cream faux fur runner, 13 by 72 inches, with a raised pattern of small trees. One runner down the middle of the table already looks like a holiday setup, and red mugs, plain white plates and a sprig of greenery finish it. It also works on a console or a buffet, and it folds flat for storage.

**A Christmas soap dispenser for the sink.** A slim white 350 ml bottle with a watercolor snowy pine print and a silver pump. It is the easiest way to bring bathroom Christmas decor into a guest bath or the kitchen without adding another object that needs dusting, and you refill it with whatever hand soap you already use.

**A Christmas door sign for the front door.** A 12 inch round red sign with a snowman in a plaid scarf and a Welcome script, hung from a hemp rope. It goes on an exterior door, an interior door or the wall by the stairs, and it stores flat in a drawer for next year.

**A C shaped side table for the sofa.** A rustic brown top on a black steel frame, about 25.6 inches tall, that slides under the sofa or a chair. A laptop, a mug and a book stay within reach, and in a small living room it replaces a bulky end table. It also works next to a bed.

**Floating shelves for one empty wall.** A set of four black metal rail shelves in sizes from about 15 to 16 inches wide. Staggered on a blank wall they hold books, a trailing plant and a frame, and the raised rail stops small items from sliding. They suit a bedroom, the space above a toilet or a kitchen wall for spices.

If you buy one, take the side table or the shelves, since they change how a room works every day. The runner, the soap dispenser and the door sign are the cheap quick wins for the weeks before the holidays.
