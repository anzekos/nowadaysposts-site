With Thanksgiving three weeks away and Christmas decorating about to start, the useful question is what you can tidy in an afternoon so the rest of the house looks calm. Three of today's picks are exactly that, and two are the holiday touches that do the most for the least effort.

**A blanket basket that hides the throw pile.** A 108 liter woven cotton rope bin, 25 by 16 by 16 inches, in a cream and oatmeal two tone. It holds the blankets, spare pillows or a toy collection beside the sofa, and it looks like decor rather than storage. The rope is soft, so it will not scratch a wood floor, and the side handles make it easy to move when you need the room cleared.

**A cable box for the power strip.** A long white box with a natural bamboo lid and a slot on each end. The power strip and the chargers go inside, the cords leave through the slots, and the desk or the TV stand stops looking like a wiring diagram. It ships with cable ties and clips for the cords that stay outside. It is the kind of fix you notice every day and nobody else notices at all.

**A lazy susan pair for the cabinet.** Two clear 360 degree turntables with a raised rim. Spices, sauces and jars sit on them, and you spin to reach the one at the back instead of emptying the shelf. They suit a corner cabinet, a pantry shelf or the fridge, and they wipe clean, which matters in the week you are cooking for a crowd.

**For the Thanksgiving table.** A set of eight napkin rings, each with a small orange pumpkin and maple leaves on a wrapped rope ring, plus eight orange and gold cloth napkins. Set them on the plates and the table looks finished without a centerpiece. The napkins wash, and the rings pack flat for next year.

**For the Christmas tree.** A 36 inch faux fur skirt in thick white plush with gold sequin snowflakes. It hides the stand and the cords and gives a green tree or a flocked one a soft base for the gifts. The slit with ties wraps around the trunk in a minute.

If you pick one, take the cable box or the lazy susans: they change how a room works every day, not just in December. The napkin rings and the tree skirt are the quick wins for the two holiday tables that are coming.
