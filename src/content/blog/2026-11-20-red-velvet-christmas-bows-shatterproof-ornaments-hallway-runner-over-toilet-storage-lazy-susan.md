Five weeks out from Christmas is when the house starts to fill up, and also when the small practical jobs get put off. This week's five cover both: two pieces that make the tree and the stairs look finished, and three that fix a hallway, a tiny bathroom and a kitchen cabinet.

**Red velvet bows for the garland and the tree.** A pack of 12 soft flocked bows, about 13 inches long and 9 inches wide, each with a twist tie on the back. One big bow every couple of feet turns a plain pine garland on a banister into something that looks planned. They also go on a wreath, a mantel garland or the front door. Tie them on slightly crooked and let the tails hang, because perfectly straight bows look stiff.

**Shatterproof ornaments.** A set of 36 plastic ball ornaments in red, green and gold, with matte, glitter, mirror and textured finishes and a string already tied on each one. Plastic is the practical choice if you have kids, a cat or a low tree. Mix the finishes, put the larger balls deep in the branches and the smaller ones near the tips, and use a few in a bowl on the table for a matching centerpiece.

**A hallway runner rug, 2x8.** A low pile gray and white runner with a faded vintage pattern, a non-slip backing and a machine washable build. Hallways take the most mud and wet shoes in winter, so a rug you can throw in the washer is easier to live with than one you spot clean. The faded pattern hides dust, and the low pile means the front door still opens over it.

**Over-the-toilet storage.** A 4 tier freestanding rack with a black metal frame, three wood shelves and an open wire basket for spare toilet paper. It adds shelf space to a small bathroom without drilling, which matters in a rental. The X brace can sit higher if your plumbing sticks out, so check that before you assemble it. Keep towels and a plant on the upper shelves and the daily things at hand height.

**A lazy susan for the cabinet.** A 2 pack of 9 inch clear turntables with a raised edge that spin 360 degrees. Spices, sauces and jars at the back of a deep cabinet come to the front with one push, so nothing expires behind the others. The same turntable works in the fridge, under the bathroom sink or on a vanity, and the second one fits a shelf of cans.

If you only buy two things this week, make it the runner and the lazy susan. They solve a daily annoyance, and they will still be in use in January when the bows and ornaments go back in the box.
