With five weeks to Christmas, the safe move is to split your decorating money in two: a couple of pieces that only work in December, and a few that stay in the house all year. This week's five do exactly that. The first two are for the mantel and the front door, the other three are small upgrades for the kitchen, the bedroom and the living room.

**Stocking holders for the mantel.** A pack of 6 heavy duty gold metal holders, each a small flat bar that sits on the mantel edge with a hook dropping down at the front. The stocking hangs from the hook, so nothing goes into the brick or the paint. They also work on a shelf, a window ledge or a counter, which helps if you do not have a fireplace. Weight is the point here: a full stocking pulls hard on a light holder, so choose a heavy one and keep the mantel edge clear.

**A pre-lit Christmas wreath for the front door.** A 24 inch pine wreath with a red bow, red berries and 55 white lights that run on batteries. No cord to a porch outlet means you can hang it on a door, a window or the wall above the mantel. Switch the lights on at dusk and check the battery box is on the back, where the door hides it, before you hang it.

**Kitchen canisters for the countertop.** A set of 4 square sage green metal canisters with a honeycomb pattern and bamboo lids, in four graduated sizes for flour, sugar, coffee and tea. Four matching containers in one color make a cluttered counter look planned. Line them up tall to small against the backsplash and keep the biggest one for the thing you use most.

**A cordless table lamp.** A small lamp with a linen drum shade and a dark wood base, rechargeable with a 4000mAh battery, with touch control and a dimmer. It goes where an outlet does not: a nightstand across the room from the wall, a dinner table, a porch table in summer. A warm dim setting at bedtime is easier on the eyes than a ceiling light.

**A round ottoman tray.** A 14.6 inch cream faux leather tray with a low rim and two stitched handles. Put two candles, a small vase and a book on it and the coffee table or ottoman looks styled; carry it to the sofa with a drink and it works as a serving tray too. Keep the grouping to three or four things so the tray does not look crowded.

If you only buy one thing before the holidays, make it the stocking holders or the wreath, because both show from the street or the sofa on day one. The canisters, the lamp and the tray are the ones that are still in use in January, when the Christmas boxes go back to the attic.
