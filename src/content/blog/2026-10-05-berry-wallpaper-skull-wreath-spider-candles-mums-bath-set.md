Fall is no longer just a porch job: people are doing it on every level of the house, so here are five finds in the order you would walk through it, from the bedroom wall to the sink.

**The wall, and a roll that warms a whole bedroom.** This peel-and-stick wallpaper is 17.5 inches wide and about 33 feet long, printed in deep navy with olive leaves and small orange berries. It goes on a headboard wall, the back of a bookcase or the top of a dresser, and it lifts off without damage, which matters if you rent. Match the pattern at each seam and one wall changes the mood of the whole room. 4.7 stars from 575.

**The door, and the wreath that wins the contest.** Halloween door contests are up 800%. This 20-inch wreath is made of black maple leaves, orange pumpkins, orange berries and two small skulls on a grapevine base. It works indoors or on a covered porch, and it looks best on a door in a light or sage colour, where the black leaves stand out. 4.4 stars from 636.

**The table, and tiny lights that do the party for you.** This set is 12 flameless tea lights, each on a black spider about 3.3 inches wide, with an orange glow and no real flame. Scatter them down a runner, along a mantel or up a staircase, and turn the big lights off. Space them unevenly and they read as a swarm rather than a row. 4.7 stars from 77.

**The porch, and mums that look full from the street.** This pack is 12 faux chrysanthemum bundles in red and orange, each about 12 inches tall and 8 inches wide, made for outdoor use. Push all of them into one big planter instead of spreading them across several small pots, because one full dome is what makes a porch look planned. 4.5 stars from 325.

**The sink, and a set that makes it look finished.** This 4-piece set has a pump dispenser, a toothbrush holder, a tumbler and a soap dish in a sandstone finish with a raised leaf pattern. The dispenser holds about 13 ounces. Keep the pieces along the back edge of the counter with a folded towel beside them, and a bare sink becomes a styled one. 4.8 stars from 228.

Start with the wall and work outward, or start with the door if you only have an hour. Either way, by the end of the week the house reads as fall from the first thing you see to the last thing you touch.
