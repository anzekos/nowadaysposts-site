There is a pattern hiding in this month's climbing searches, and it is not the obvious one. Yes, everything has the word *fall* in front of it — plaid wallpaper up 600%, dining table decor up 400%, entry table decor up 400% — but look at what people are actually saving inside those searches and almost none of it is a renovation. It peels off. It plugs in. It rests on the rim of the tub. Bathroom decor ideas are up 300% on last year and bedazzling ideas up 3000%, and the pieces winning both are the ones you can install on a Tuesday evening and undo on a Saturday. Five below, one per trend, and four of them need permission from nobody.

**Fall plaid wallpaper, up 600% this month, and the version that comes back off.** Plaid on the wall is the fall look of the season, and it used to mean paste, a table, and a deposit you were not getting back. This is peel-and-stick vinyl in a soft neutral gingham — a cream ground with thin taupe lines in a fine, dense check rather than a big country windowpane — and the roll runs 393 inches long by 17.3 wide, enough for the wall behind a headboard or the back panel of a bookcase. The pattern is drawn so the lines meet edge to edge, which is the whole game with a check: line the first strip up properly and the seams stop existing. It is thicker than the dollar-store contact papers, so it does not stretch as you smooth it, it wipes clean, and it lifts off in one piece when the lease ends. 4.3 stars from 29.

**Fall dining table decor, up 400%, and the trick that is always height.** Every autumn tablescape that gets saved is doing the same quiet thing: it goes *up*. Flat tables photograph flat, and no amount of extra bowls fixes it. This is three cast-iron candlesticks in an antique brass finish at 11.8, 13.8 and 15.4 inches, which is the point — the staggered heights make them read as one arrangement instead of three objects that happen to be near each other. The bases are weighted and three inches wide, so nobody knocks one over reaching for the salt, and the cups take ordinary 7/8-inch tapers you can buy anywhere. Run them down the centre of the table for dinner, and when the season turns they move to the mantel and do the same job there. 4.7 stars from 404.

**Bathroom decor ideas, up 300% on last year, and the object in every one of those photos.** Scroll the bathrooms being saved right now and there is a tray across the tub in most of them. This one is honey-toned bamboo and expands from 28.5 to 40 inches, so it grips the rim of nearly any tub rather than the one specific tub it was designed for. The middle panel folds up into a stand that holds a paperback or a tablet at an angle you can actually read from lying down, a slot on the underside takes a wine glass by the stem so it cannot tip, and two side trays slide out for a candle and a bar of soap. Silicone pads underneath keep it still on wet enamel, and the whole thing folds flat and slides behind the door. 4.6 stars from 217.

**Fall entry table decor, up 400%, and what to do when there is no room for an entry table.** The console-and-bench entryway everyone pins needs about four feet of clear floor, and most hallways do not have it. This is 35 inches of wall doing both jobs instead: a narrow shelf across the top for a lamp, a frame and the post, heavy double hooks below it for coats and bags, and a strip of small hooks for keys, which is the part that quietly changes the mornings. Rustic brown wood in a black metal frame, pre-assembled and pre-drilled, and it mounts high enough that a basket for shoes still slides in underneath. It is the only piece here that needs a drill, and it needs it for about ten minutes. 4.6 stars from 560.

**Bedazzling ideas, up 3000% on last year, and the ceiling nobody is allowed to touch.** The reason most bedrooms never get the chandelier is not taste or money, it is the junction box. This one plugs into a wall socket: a clear cord runs from the fitting up to a small swag hook, across the ceiling and down to the outlet, with an on/off switch on the line and 236 inches of cord to route it however the room demands. The frame is white with real glass crystals — teardrops under the arms, bead strands between them — so once the lamps are on at night it throws small points of light across the wall, which is the entire appeal of the search term. At 17 inches across it suits a bedroom corner or a small dining nook without dominating it, and any E12 bulb fits. 4.7 stars from 140.

Five trends, and the same idea running underneath all of them: the searches are climbing not because people want to renovate, but because they have found the version of each upgrade that does not ask anyone's permission. Wallpaper that peels off. Candlesticks that just stand there. A tray that rests on the rim. A chandelier on an extension cord. The room in the photograph stopped being a project and became a Tuesday evening — which is exactly why it is up 600%.
